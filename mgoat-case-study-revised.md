How do you tell a good Maximo story?

  

Starting with the numbers. IBM Maximo is a 40+ years old asset lifecycle management software suite.

  

It’s being used by 7,200 companies worldwide to manage assets, manufacturing, maintenance, and running the world.

  

Being 40+ years old means Maximo has a really steep learning curve. And it’s not going to change anytime soon, considering how many clients respected and were used to the way it is.

  

Our research and intelligence has shown that:

90% of clients use only 10% of it.

  

The opportunity is you can utilize at least 10-15% more of Maximo, to get 40% more ROI benefit. But…that sounds like a huge step for big clients with complex operations.

  

That is the business problem we’re trying to solve, with the Maximo Growth Opportunities Assessment tool.

  

The initial purpose we had in mind was to help clients know where they are with their Maximo operations, and IBM client teams drive better utilization & expansion, to help them reach their goals and ROI faster.

  

How we broke down complexity into manageable steps:

  

Maximo has 13 core applications (modules)

And many pathways tailored to each industry (manufacturing, transportation, oil & gas, nuclear, etc…)

  
  

We identified 76 milestones to reach full value with Maximo. Each milestone has 5-10 core actions, involving roles like supervisors, technicians, admins, and so on.

  

This was the hard and painful part. We even had to build a journey simulator to validate these by hand. (Thanks to Tanushri for the concept, visual accompanying in text frame)

  

And we mapped them into initially 40 questions, and we launched the first demo in MaximoWorld in Nashville, TN to get the first round of usage and feedback.

  

[1st version Visuals + Feedback]

  

After that, we refined the assessment and the recommendation engine for the clients. This version was shown and demoed at the Maximo Advisory Council in Lowell, MA

  

[Assessment visuals]

  

I thought, “this will need to be simpler”. As a lot of customers don’t know about the listed concepts.

  

Then, the revised assessment was cut down from 40 to 10 core questions. I and my colleagues went back to Figma to define the visuals, then the question flow, before building it again with IBM Bob (our AI coding agent).

  

The customer now would focus first on their business goals, and their challenges, before being asked how they are using Maximo and their operational details, to reduce cognitive load.

  

[Report]

  

After that, they get the next steps report. This was built with 3 acts so that an executive can scan it within 20 seconds, and their teams to know what to act on.

I aligned this to build trust first (acknowledging what the client has already achieved) before introducing gaps and recommendations.

  

The structure was also built with progressive disclosure in mind, to hide complexity until it is needed, and clear actions will be surfaced once they read it.

  

[APM and FSM card graphics]

We needed to show the value of expansion.

For each maturity stage, I made sure that every action displays clear financial impact, benefits and ROI metrics (based on IBM Institute for Business Value) so the reader has instant proof to secure leadership buy-in.

  

[Next steps card]

In the next action steps, the system highlights a single top priority alongside two secondary steps so you always know where to start.

  

Each step explicitly tags responsible roles, like Asset Manager or Sys Admin, making it clear as to how to set up Maximo tasks with your team.

  

[APM and FSM maturity maps page]

To give leaders clarity without forcing two different systems onto one scale, I split asset management into two parallel paths: one for tracking equipment health and one for managing technicians in the field.

Stages 1 to 5 for each path helps executives pinpoint exact gaps in their hardware monitoring, versus on-the-ground execution.
